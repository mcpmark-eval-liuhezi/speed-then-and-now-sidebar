## Art Corner: 'Chariots were the first speed machines.'
**VERIFIED ✓** — The Metropolitan Museum of Art's collection includes the famous Assyrian
**Chariot with Scenes from the Epic of Gilgamesh** (ca. 645–635 B.C., from Kouyunjik, Iraq,
State of Assyria). Museum object number 25.78.133, Curatorial Department: Greek and Roman Art.
Maker/Culture: **Assyrian (State of Assyria)**. Date: **ca. 645–635 B.C.**

That's roughly 3,000 years before the first Formula 1 car crossed the line at Silverstone.

## History: 'It all started at Silverstone in 1950.'
**VERIFIED ✓** — Your draft line is correct. The very first Formula 1 World Championship
race was the **1950 British Grand Prix at Silverstone**, held in May 1950 and won by
Giuseppe "Nino" Farina in an Alfa Romeo, in front of watching King George VI. Source
results from Wikipedia's Silverstone Circuit page, Silverstone.co.uk's official history,
and Motorsport Magazine all confirm: the 1950 British Grand Prix at Silverstone was the
first race in the newly created World Championship of Drivers.
